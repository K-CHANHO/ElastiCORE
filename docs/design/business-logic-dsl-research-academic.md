# Research: Academic Literature on Business-Logic DSLs (for ElastiCORE)

## 1. Model-Driven Engineering Classics

**Key findings:**
- **Executable UML / fUML / Alf**: fUML gives precise execution semantics to a subset of UML (activities, classes, composite structures); Alf is a textual action language mapped onto fUML's abstract syntax, effectively making UML "implementation-quality." (Springer, *Software and Systems Modeling*, "On the automated translational execution of the action language for foundational UML," https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10270-016-0556-7; overview: https://modeling-languages.com/new-executable-uml-standards-fuml-and-alf/)
- **OCL**: Warmer & Kleppe, *The Object Constraint Language: Precise Modeling with UML* (Addison-Wesley, 1999; 2nd ed. "Getting Your Models Ready for MDA," 2003). OCL is a side-effect-free declarative constraint language layered on UML for invariants, pre/post-conditions. Retrospective: "Object Constraint Language (OCL): past, present and future," *ACM SIGSOFT Software Engineering Notes* 36(1), 2011, https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/1921532.1921543.
- **Fowler's *Domain-Specific Languages*** (Addison-Wesley, 2010, with Rebecca Parsons): frames DSL design as requiring understanding of domain *behavior*, not just concepts — what a construct does when executed, not merely what it structurally represents. https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.5555/1809745
- **Language workbenches**: Erdweg et al., "Evaluating and Comparing Language Workbenches" and "The State of the Art in Language Workbenches" (CWI, http://homepages.cwi.nl/~storm/publications/lwc13paper.pdf, lwc13-comlan.pdf). MPS (projectional editing, mixing textual/tabular/graphical notations) vs. textual workbenches Xtext/Spoofax/Rascal (grammar-driven, better tooling reuse). Xtext/Sirius integration for hybrid graphical-textual DSLs is an active concern: "Engineering Hybrid Graphical-Textual Languages with Sirius and Xtext" (MODELS'19/'24 lineage), https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3652620.3688346.
- **Why behavior DSLs struggled**: Lancia/Whittle et al., "The State of Practice in Model-Driven Engineering" (2013/2014, https://eprints.lancs.ac.uk/id/eprint/69765/1/SO_SW_2012_12_0188.R1_Whittle.pdf) found no industry consensus on how to specify *behavior* in DSLs — some use UML behavioral diagrams, some Abstract State Machines, most invent bespoke behavioral sub-languages, causing fragmentation. Separately, business-rule-specific work notes existing approaches "fail at supporting rules that are both high-level (domain terms) and operational (automatically executable)" — the perennial gap between readable and executable.

**What worked/failed and why**: Structural DSLs (data/entity modeling — ElastiCORE's current strength) succeeded broadly because metamodels map cleanly to code generation templates. Behavioral DSLs succeeded only when tied to a narrow, well-understood execution semantics (fUML/Alf, OCL) — attempts at general "business logic in UML" (full xUML tools) saw limited industrial uptake due to tooling complexity and the difficulty of round-tripping generated + hand-written code.

**Implication**: A YAML business-logic DSL should pick ONE narrow, well-defined behavioral semantics (e.g., contracts + a state-machine + decision tables) rather than inventing a general-purpose action language — general action languages are where MDE behavioral DSLs historically got bogged down.

## 2. Behaviour Formalisms as DSL Backbone

**Key findings:**
- **Harel Statecharts**: D. Harel, "Statecharts: A Visual Formalism for Complex Systems," *Science of Computer Programming* 8(3), 1987, pp. 231–274 (https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0167642387900359; full PDF: https://dubroy.com/refs/Statecharts_a_visual_formalism_for_complex_systems.pdf). Adds hierarchy, concurrency, communication to FSMs. **SCXML** (W3C) operationalizes this in XML and has documented business (not just protocol) uses — Apache Commons SCXML "BusinessCases" wiki reports companies applying it directly to business-process logic (https://cwiki.apache.org/confluence/display/COMMONS/SCXML+BusinessCases).
- **Petri nets / workflow nets**: van der Aalst, "The Application of Petri Nets to Workflow Management," *Journal of Circuits, Systems and Computers* 8(1), 1998, pp. 21–66 (https://www.vdaalst.rwth-aachen.de/publications/p153.pdf); also "Making Work Flow: On the Application of Petri Nets to Business Process Management," ICATPN 2002 (https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/3-540-48068-4_1). Workflow nets have clean executable ("token game") semantics and enable soundness verification (no deadlocks/dead code) — this is the strongest track record of a formalism used directly for BUSINESS process logic at scale (BPM tooling, ProM).
- **Design by Contract**: B. Meyer, "Applying 'Design by Contract'," *IEEE Computer*, 1992 (https://se.inf.ethz.ch/~meyer/publications/computer/contract.pdf). Pre/post/invariant triad, implemented coherently only in Eiffel; widely borrowed conceptually (JML, .NET Code Contracts) but rarely as *first-class business-rule* expression — mostly used for defensive programming/API correctness, not domain rules per se.
- **TLA+ / Alloy / Event-B**: TLA+ favors temporal/protocol properties (Lamport; "Why Amazon Chose TLA+," https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-662-43652-3_3); Alloy favors structural/relational properties via SAT-based model finding; Event-B explicitly claims applicability to "processes, procedures, and business rules" not just hardware/software (per search results on the Landing Gear System case study, arXiv:2112.15147). Comparative: "Alloy meets TLA+: An exploratory study," arXiv:1603.03599. These are heavyweight verification tools, not authoring DSLs — used to *check* specs, not typically as the business-facing surface syntax.

**What worked and why**: Statecharts/workflow nets succeeded for business logic because they map to a visual, business-comprehensible artifact (a diagram) AND have precise executable semantics — dual legibility to domain experts and machines. TLA+/Alloy/Event-B remain expert tools; they verify but are not readable by business stakeholders, so they've stayed adjacent to (not embedded in) business-rule authoring.

**Implication**: For a YAML DSL, a restricted statechart/workflow-net-style state-transition block (states + guarded transitions + entry/exit actions) is the best-precedented way to capture use-case behavior; reserve TLA+/Alloy-style tools (if at all) for offline verification of generated logic, not as the authoring surface.

## 3. Business Rules Research

**Key findings:**
- **Business Rules Manifesto** (Ron Ross et al., Business Rules Group, 2003): https://www.brcommunity.com/articles.php?id=b178. Core tenet: rules are a first-class asset separate from process/data, expressed in terms business people understand, and should be declarative not procedural.
- **SBVR** (OMG, adopted Sept. 2005): "Semantics of Business Vocabulary and Business Rules" — first OMG standard for fact models + rules as a highly structured conceptual framework (not a concrete syntax). Its complexity is widely cited as *why* it underperformed adoption expectations: "the complexity of the SBVR metamodel prevents its broad and rapid usage" (per academic works reducing SBVR to usable subsets, e.g. Bonais et al., *Applied Ontology* 2016, https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3233/AO-160162).
- **DMN / FEEL**: Calvanese, Dumas, Laurson, Maggi, Montali, Teinemaa, "Semantics and Analysis of DMN Decision Tables," BPM 2016 (arXiv:1603.07466, https://arxiv.org/pdf/1603.07466) — gives formal semantics to DMN tables plus **scalable algorithms for overlap and completeness (missing-rule) detection**, using a geometric hyper-rectangle representation of rules. This is directly actionable for a decision-table feature: verification is tractable and well-studied.
- **Decision table comprehensibility**: Multiple empirical studies (e.g., "An empirical evaluation of the comprehensibility of decision table, tree and rule based predictive models," *Decision Support Systems*, https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1016/j.dss.2010.12.003) find decision tables **outperform** decision trees and free-form rules on comprehension accuracy AND are preferred by end users — strong empirical case for decision tables as a business-facing construct.
- **Rete / Drools**: Forgy's Rete algorithm (PhD thesis 1978–79; simplified paper 1982) underlies production-rule engines like Drools (ReteOO). Rete-style forward-chaining engines are good at large rule sets with complex cross-fact matching but have a well-known debuggability/maintainability cost (non-obvious execution order, emergent behavior) — a frequently cited reason enterprises retreat from pure production-rule engines toward decision tables/DMN for simpler cases.
- **Datalog**: A declarative, Horn-clause, guaranteed-terminating logic language, order-independent (unlike Prolog) — theoretically attractive for business rules because its purely declarative, monotonic semantics make static analysis (including the overlap/completeness style checks above) easier than Rete-style production rules.

**What worked/failed and why**: DMN/decision-tables succeeded because they are small, bounded, and formally checkable (completeness/overlap decidable) — the sweet spot between expressive power and analyzability. SBVR and pure production-rule systems struggled: SBVR from meta-model complexity, Rete/Drools from opaque execution order despite raw power.

**Implication**: Favor DMN-style decision tables (with FEEL-like small expression language) for the "rule" portion of the business-logic DSL — they have the best evidence base for both human comprehension AND automatic verification (overlap/completeness), directly reusable for LLM-authored or LLM-reviewed rules.

## 4. Event-Driven / DDD Formalisms

**Key findings:**
- **Event Storming** (Alberto Brandolini, *Introducing EventStorming*, self-published/Leanpub, ~2013–ongoing): a workshop notation (orange stickies = domain events), explicitly **not** formally specified — practitioner methodology, not peer-reviewed formalism (confirmed by search: "presented primarily as a practical methodology rather than as a formal academic paper with mathematical formalization"). https://www.eventstorming.com/book/
- **DDD academic uptake**: Özkan & Babur, "Domain-Driven Design in Software Development: A Systematic Literature Review on Implementation, Challenges, and Effectiveness," arXiv:2310.01905 (v4, 2025), 36 peer-reviewed studies surveyed — confirms Event Storming's role in discovering bounded contexts/aggregates but notes a shortage of formal/empirical evaluation of DDD techniques generally. Also: "DDD constructs are explicitly formalized in DDD-centric DSLs such as Context Mapper (CML) and LEMMA DDML" — i.e., existing formalization efforts are DSL-based, not mathematical-formalism-based, which is directly relevant precedent for ElastiCORE's own approach.
- **Event formalization (general, not DDD-specific)**: "Event-Model-F," based on foundational ontology DOLCE+DnS Ultralight, addresses interoperability gaps in event semantics (causality, correlation, time/space) — https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/1597735.1597760. Heavier-weight than needed for a code-gen DSL but useful as a checklist of what a "domain event" concept should minimally carry (causation, correlation id, time).
- **Sagas**: Garcia-Molina & Salem, "Sagas," *ACM SIGMOD* 1987 (https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/38713.38742) — the foundational formalization: a long-lived transaction (LLT) as a sequence of sub-transactions each with a compensating transaction, guaranteeing "all complete or all compensated." 792+ citations; this is the canonical reference underlying every modern saga/process-manager pattern.
- **Choreography vs. orchestration**: formal treatments exist via WS-CDL/WS-BPEL trace semantics and projection (per search results referencing academic proposals of "formal languages for both choreography and orchestration with trace semantics"); practically, orchestration = single-party controller view, choreography = global multi-party message view, with orchestration provably a valid "projection" of a choreography in some formalizations.

**What worked/failed and why**: Event Storming succeeded as a *discovery* tool precisely because it deliberately avoids formalism (sticky notes, human-scale); it was never meant to be executable. Sagas succeeded as a formal backbone because the compensating-transaction model is simple, composable, and has clean failure semantics — this is the most mature, directly reusable formalism in this whole area for a "process/workflow" DSL section.

**Implication**: Model ElastiCORE's process/saga constructs directly on Garcia-Molina's compensating-transaction semantics (each step + its compensation, declared declaratively) rather than inventing new failure semantics; treat Event Storming as informing the *vocabulary* (events/commands/aggregates) rather than as an executable formalism itself.

## 5. Behavioural/Session Types & Effect Systems

**Key findings:**
- Session types (Honda et al. lineage) and effect systems are formally shown to be **mutually expressible**: "Effects as sessions, sessions as effects," POPL 2016 (https://dl.acm.org/doi/abs/10.1145/2837614.2837634) — session types (from π-calculus, protocol-oriented) and effect systems (from λ-calculus, action-oriented) can each encode the other. This is directly relevant: it means a declarative "effects" section (this use case reads Entity X, writes Entity Y, calls Service Z) is theoretically equivalent to a lightweight session/protocol type.
- Survey: Ancona et al., "Behavioral Types in Programming Languages," *Foundations and Trends in Programming Languages* (https://www.irif.fr/~gc/papers/soar.pdf) — comprehensive taxonomy of how behavioral/effect typing statically constrains what a piece of code may do.
- Capability-based declarative permission systems: "Declarative, Temporal, and Practical Programming with Capabilities" (Cambridge CTSRD, capweave — https://www.cl.cam.ac.uk/research/security/ctsrd/pdfs/201305-temporal-practical-capabilities.pdf) demonstrates converting a plain program + a declarative temporal policy into a capability-respecting one — a template for "declare allowed reads/writes/calls, have the generator/compiler enforce it."

**What worked/failed and why**: These are primarily PL-theory tools (used in Rust-like effect trackers, actor/protocol verification), not business-rule tools — no evidence of large-scale *business-logic* adoption, but the theory is directly portable to a generator: an "effects:" YAML block per use-case (reads/writes/calls) is essentially a lightweight, checkable behavioral type, and the equivalence result means you don't need full session-type machinery — a simple declared-capability list is already expressive enough for most compile-time enforcement purposes.

**Implication**: Add a declarative `effects:` (reads/writes/emits/calls) block per service method — grounded in the session-types≡effects equivalence, this is cheap to implement (static capability list) and gives real leverage: the generator can statically verify a use case's Java implementation doesn't touch undeclared repositories/services.

## 6. LLMs, DSLs, and Verification (2023–2026)

**Key findings:**
- **DSLs materially help LLM reliability.** Joshi, "DSLs Enable Reliable Use of LLMs," martinfowler.com, July 2026 (https://martinfowler.com/articles/llm-and-dsls.html): DSLs' constrained vocabulary means few-shot examples generalize reliably, and DSLs typically ship parsers/validators enabling autonomous self-correction; argues the LLM is best used to *co-design* the DSL, then treated as a reliable interface to it once the abstraction is stable.
- **Quantified evidence, DSL vs. general code**: Ayoughi, Dewar, Nejati, Sabetzadeh, "DSL or Code? Evaluating the Quality of LLM-Generated Algebraic Specifications: A Case Study in Optimization at Kinaxis," ICSE-SEIP 2026, arXiv:2601.00469 (https://arxiv.org/html/2601.00469). With structuring + iterative refinement: **AMPL DSL executability 94.7% vs. Python 79.8%** (reasoning LLMs, public benchmark, 60+6 problems, 4 LLMs, 5 reps, 11,500+ specs); correctness comparable or slightly better for the DSL (0.48 vs 0.49 relative error; 65% vs 58% exact solutions). Conclusion: DSL generation is "as reliable ... and sometimes better" than Python, refuting the concern that low-representation DSLs disadvantage LLMs when paired with structuring + refinement loops.
- **Counter-evidence exists too**: "Imperative vs. Declarative Programming Paradigms for Open-Universe Scene Generation," arXiv:2504.05482, found the *opposite* in a different domain — long/complex DSL documentation increased LLM confusion vs. plain imperative code. And "From Imperative to Declarative: Towards LLM-friendly..." arXiv:2510.04607 explores converting imperative specs to declarative ones specifically to *help* LLMs, implying naive declarative syntax alone isn't sufficient — DSL design quality (small vocabulary, good examples) matters more than declarative-vs-imperative per se.
- **Grammar-constrained decoding**: SynCode (arXiv:2403.01632) and "Decode-Time Grammars" (arXiv:2607.18357) constrain token generation to a CFG in real time; the latter specifically targets low-resource DSLs/custom APIs where even grammar-constrained decoding alone still produces invalid references (needs environment-typed slots), suggesting grammar constraints must be paired with symbol-table/schema awareness (relevant to ElastiCORE's namespace/DSL grammar).
- **Spec-driven development**: Thoughtworks (2025) and GitHub Spec Kit (Sept. 2025) popularize "spec is the source of truth, code is generated/verifiable artifact." Academic formalization: "Understanding Specification-Driven Code Generation with LLMs: An Empirical Study Design," arXiv:2601.03878. TICODER (interactive test-driven intent clarification) is cited as a concrete instantiation.
- **LLM + formal verification loops**: "A Benchmark for Vericoding: Formally Verified Program Synthesis," arXiv:2509.22908 (POPL 2026) — 12,504 specs across Dafny/Verus/Lean; **success rates: Dafny 82%, Verus 44%, Lean 27%** with off-the-shelf LLMs — Dafny's uniform math-like types make LLM-generated verified code far more tractable than Verus's split ghost/native-type system or Lean's general theorem-proving surface. Directly relevant lesson: verification-friendliness of the target formalism strongly gates how well an LLM can produce verifiably-correct code from it.

**What worked/failed and why**: The recurring theme is that DSLs help LLMs *when the DSL is small, well-exampled, and has a fast validator/compiler for self-correction*; they can hurt when the DSL's own surface complexity/documentation burden exceeds what a general-purpose language would need. Verification-oriented targets (Dafny) outperform ones with more type-system subtlety (Verus, Lean) — simplicity of the target semantics predicts both human and LLM success.

**Implication**: Keep ElastiCORE's business-logic DSL small and uniform (favor one or two composable primitives — decision tables + guarded state machines + declared effects — over a general action language), and ensure the generator gives fast syntactic/semantic validation feedback (a parser + the DMN-style overlap/completeness checker) so an LLM authoring the YAML can self-correct, mirroring the Kinaxis/Dafny results.

## Ranked List: 8 Most Decision-Relevant Sources

1. **Calvanese et al., "Semantics and Analysis of DMN Decision Tables," BPM 2016** (arXiv:1603.07466) — formal DMN semantics + practical overlap/completeness verification algorithms; most directly implementable for a `rules:`/decision-table DSL feature.
2. **Ayoughi et al., "DSL or Code? ... Kinaxis," ICSE-SEIP 2026** (arXiv:2601.00469) — hard accuracy numbers (94.7% vs 79.8% executability) showing a well-designed DSL beats general code for LLM authoring when paired with structuring/refinement.
3. **Garcia-Molina & Salem, "Sagas," ACM SIGMOD 1987** (https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/38713.38742) — canonical, simple, composable formalism for the process/compensation part of the DSL.
4. **Harel, "Statecharts: A Visual Formalism for Complex Systems," 1987** (https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0167642387900359) — best-precedented dual-legible (human + executable) behavior formalism for aggregate/use-case lifecycle.
5. **van der Aalst, "The Application of Petri Nets to Workflow Management," 1998** (https://www.vdaalst.rwth-aachen.de/publications/p153.pdf) — strongest track record of a formalism used directly for verifiable *business* process logic, not just protocols.
6. **Whittle et al., "The State of Practice in Model-Driven Engineering," 2013/2014** (https://eprints.lancs.ac.uk/id/eprint/69765/1/SO_SW_2012_12_0188.R1_Whittle.pdf) — explains empirically why behavior DSLs fragmented in MDE practice; cautionary guidance for scope discipline.
7. **"Effects as Sessions, Sessions as Effects," POPL 2016** (https://dl.acm.org/doi/abs/10.1145/2837614.2837634) — theoretical grounding for a lightweight declarative `effects:` (reads/writes/calls) block.
8. **"A Benchmark for Vericoding: Formally Verified Program Synthesis," POPL 2026** (arXiv:2509.22908) — shows verification-friendliness of the target formalism (Dafny-style uniform types) strongly predicts LLM success; argues for semantic simplicity in the DSL's expression sublanguage.

**Uncertainty flags**: (a) SBVR/DDD-specific formal papers are thin — most DDD "formalization" is itself DSL-based (Context Mapper/LEMMA), not mathematical, so claims there rest on secondary characterizations rather than primary formal papers I directly verified. (b) The imperative-vs-declarative LLM-reliability evidence is mixed across domains (Kinaxis favors DSL; open-universe scene generation arXiv:2504.05482 favors imperative) — treat "DSLs help LLMs" as domain- and design-quality-contingent, not a universal law. (c) Some sources (SCXML BusinessCases wiki, Event-Model-F) are secondary/gray literature, not peer-reviewed, and are flagged as such above.
